# Obsidian

The silt had dried into a crust that cracked under her boots, and beneath it the field was still soft. She found the stone on the third morning of clearing the lower wall, half buried where the gate had been. It was black and smooth and as cold as well water, and when she held it up to the light it was'nt black at all but the deep green of an old bottle. ^stone

She thought of [[Chapter 01 - The Flood|the night of the flod]], of the rope going soft in her brother's hands, and of the summer that came after. Nothing in either had told her that a river could carry glass.

## The leter

The letter came in October with the carrier from [[Millers Lane]], folded small and gone soft at the corners. Her brother had never wrote a letter in his life. She read it standing up.

> Mara. I am in a city with more people in one street than in the whole valley. There is <u>wrok</u> here for anyone who will carry things, and I carry things. They pay $5 a week and $2 for the bed in Room #4, which leaves me 3 dollars and alot of evenings. I think of the field. Do not sell the ewes.
> Your brother<br>T.

She read it twice more and put it under the stone.
