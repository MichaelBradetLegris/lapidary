The field came back in August, brown and flattened, smelling of something that had died slowly. Mara walked the fence line twice before she would let herself look at it properly.

Her brother had left in June. He had not said where, and she had not asked, because asking would of made it a departure rather than an errand, and she was not ready for that.

The ewes were fine. The ewes were always fine. It was the one mercy of that whole year, and she took it without gratitude, the way you take a thing you are owed.
