# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

Work towards 1.0.

### Added

- Dark mode. The app follows your computer's light or dark setting, and the window's title bar follows with it.
- An Appearance setting in Settings: Match system, Light or Dark. The window switches as soon as you choose, and the choice is kept for the next start.
- Reviews follow their chapter. When you rename or move a chapter in Obsidian, or move the whole vault, open the chapter again from the list and its reviews come with it; a card says where they were. A renamed or moved chapter is recognised by its text, so this works only when the text has not changed since the app last read it. A chapter in a moved vault is recognised by its file name and the name of its folder.
- The review dialog says when your Claude Code is older than 2.1.283 and may not offer the newest models. It shows `claude update` ready to copy, the same command by full path for a terminal that does not find `claude`, and Check again.
- On a Claude Pro plan, a new user gets Sonnet preselected once, when Claude is found ready while the welcome guide is open for the first time; showing the guide again from Settings never changes the model. The review dialog notes that Opus models, Default included, use your limits fastest.
- A review that fails because Claude Code is out of date or its sign-in has ended now also checks Claude again and opens the Setup screen, as a missing or signed-out Claude Code already did.
- The status bar tells more Claude states apart: not installed, out of date, not signed in, sign-in ended, usage limit reached, could not check, and ready through an API key.
- A log file, `logs/main.log` in the app's data folder, records when the app starts and quits; what each check of Claude or ChatGPT found, such as the Claude Code version and how it signs in; each review and each save, as sizes, counts and times; requests that failed; and problems it works around, such as a settings file it could not read. It never holds the text of your chapters, the guidelines, prompts or replies. It is rotated at 1 MiB, and keys, email addresses and your home folder are scrubbed from it.
- The MIT licence.
- Third-party notices. The installed app's `resources` folder holds `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`, which lists Electron and every package the app includes, each with the licence it comes with, and `LICENSE.txt`, the app's own MIT licence.
- Ctrl+S saves to the chapter, as Save to chapter does. It does nothing while a dialog, a question or Settings is open, or when there is nothing to save. In a paragraph box it says to press Done first, and with the suggestion editor open it says to press the editor's Save first.
- Backups. Before every Save to chapter, the version of the chapter it replaces is copied to the app's data folder, into `backups`, in a folder of that chapter's own, named by the time of the save. For each chapter, the copies from the last 20 saves are kept, plus the first of each day for 30 days. If the copy cannot be made, nothing is written and a card says why. Backups never go into your manuscript folder.
- Show backups, in the toolbar's Actions menu while a chapter is open, opens that chapter's backup folder in File Explorer with the newest backup selected; the "Saved to the chapter file." card has the same button. When the chapter has no backups at its present path, a card says so instead, with Show all backups: backups made before you renamed or moved the chapter, or moved the vault, stay in a folder of their own, named after the old file name. The card goes once a Save to chapter makes a backup. Settings has a Backups row that shows where backups are kept, with Show in folder and a note on what is kept.
- The status bar counts the changes the chapter file does not have yet, as in "3 not saved to chapter", in orange, beside the pending, accepted and dismissed counts.
- The first time a change is waiting to be saved, a note hangs under the toolbar, over the top of the side panel, and points at Save to chapter: your decisions are kept in the review, not in the chapter yet, and Save to chapter (Ctrl+S) writes them into the chapter file it names, after backing up the version it replaces. It stays clear of the toolbar's buttons and of the message cards: with the side panel hidden or folded, it hangs over the top right of the chapter, below any message cards there. It waits while the Setup screen or the welcome guide is open. Got it, or your first Save to chapter, puts the note away for good. The page shown when nothing is open, and the question Accept all asks, now also say that nothing reaches the chapter until you press Save to chapter.
- The chapter list puts the same orange dot before every chapter with changes its file does not have yet, so you can see which chapters still need Save to chapter. The dot stays when you open another chapter or restart the app, and goes once the chapter is saved. Point at it to see how many changes are waiting.
- Paragraphs you are editing are kept. What you type in a paragraph box is saved in the app's data folder within a second, and the box comes back with your text the next time you open the chapter after the app was closed, updated or crashed. If the paragraph has changed in the meantime, the box does not come back; your text is kept in a .txt file in the app's data folder instead, and a card's Show the text button opens it. If another program holds the saved boxes open when you open the chapter, a card says so and nothing is written over them until they can be read.
- A handle between the suggestions list and the comments in the side panel. Drag it up or down to give either more room, so that once the suggestions are dealt with the comments can have most of the panel. With the keyboard, Tab to it and move it with the up and down arrows, or with Home and End to the limits; a double-click on it, or Enter, puts back the usual split. Each side keeps room for three rows when the panel is tall enough for both. In a panel too short for that, the suggestions take no more than their usual share, and where the handle could hardly move it is a plain line that does nothing. The split is kept for the next start. In a window too short for two parts, where the side panel scrolls as one, there is no handle.
- Handles on the inner edges of the chapter list and the side panel. Drag either to make its column wider or narrower; a column cannot be made so wide that it squeezes the chapter's text. With the keyboard, Tab to a handle and move it with the left and right arrows, or with Home and End to the narrowest and widest; a double-click on it, or Enter, puts back the usual width. The widths are kept for the next start as shares of the window, so a column keeps its share when you resize the window or zoom. In a window too narrow for them, the columns narrow and fold to strips as before, and where a handle could hardly move it is a plain line that does nothing.
- A small caret at the top of each of those handles hides the chapter list or the side panel, and the chapter's text takes the room. A hidden column leaves a slim strip at the edge of the window; click it, or the caret at its top, to bring the column back at the width it had. With the keyboard, Tab to a caret and press Enter or Space. A hidden column stays hidden at the next start, and in a window too narrow for the columns it folds to its strip as before.
- A new Setup screen for Claude. A card at its top says what the last check found, such as Claude Code not installed, not signed in, out of date, or ready and with which account, and has Check again. Below it are the steps for your system, Windows, macOS or Linux: open a terminal, run one command, come back. For a first install that command installs Claude Code and signs you in, in one line; to sign in again it is `claude auth login`, and for an old Claude Code `claude update`, each with the same command by its full path, on your own system's tab, for a terminal that does not find `claude`. Troubleshooting answers the usual questions, Advanced shows which Claude Code was found and its version, and a note at the foot says that Lapidary never sees your Claude password or sign-in token. The review dialog's note about an out-of-date Claude Code shows there too.
- Coming back to Lapidary from a terminal checks Claude again by itself while the Setup screen has something for you to do, at most every 3 seconds, so there is no need to press Check again.
- At start-up, when Claude is your provider and Claude Code is not installed, not signed in, out of date or could not be checked, a card says so, with Open Setup. It goes by itself once Claude is ready, when you open Setup, or when you switch the provider to ChatGPT, and once closed it stays closed.
- Test the connection, on the Setup screen beside Check again, sends Claude one very small request through your Claude Code, as a review would, and says under the buttons whether it went through, how long it took and which model answered, or why it did not. It counts against your plan like any other request, or is billed to your API key or through your cloud provider when Claude Code signs in with one of those, as the note under the button says, so it runs only when you press it, and it gives up after 60 seconds. When a review said your sign-in had ended and you have signed in again since, a test that goes through clears that, as a review that goes through does. Test the connection and Check again keep the keyboard focus while they run, so you need not Tab back to them from the top of the window.
- Review with AI says when the style sheet in your guidelines file is still empty: "Your style sheet is still empty: spelling variant, character names, invented words. Without it the reviewer may "fix" them." The note goes once you have filled it in and come back to Lapidary.
- Shift+J and Shift+K go to the next and previous pending suggestion, passing over the ones you have decided, and wrap round at the end of the chapter. The focused suggestion's box mentions Shift+J when nothing is focused.
- A welcome guide on the first start, in place of the usual view, with three steps that each say whether they are done. Connect your manuscript folder says what Lapidary does with it: it lists the .md and .txt files in it as chapters, changes a chapter only when you press Save to chapter, and adds one file of its own, GUIDELINES.md; under it, two optional rows say where reviews and backups are kept. Connect AI agents, which is optional, has a part for Claude, saying what the check of Claude Code found, with Set up Claude, which opens the Setup screen, and one for ChatGPT, saying whether you are signed in, with Sign in with ChatGPT. Fill in your style sheet, also optional, has Create and open, which makes GUIDELINES.md from the template and opens it in your computer's app for .md files and reads Open once the file is there, and Open folder, which shows it in File Explorer; it is done once the style sheet holds something, and checks again when you come back to Lapidary. Start, once a folder is chosen, and Skip close the guide for good, and Settings has Show the welcome guide again. If closing it cannot be saved, a card says so, and the guide stays closed until Lapidary next starts. Settings from an earlier version that name a manuscript folder do not show it.
- Review with AI says when a chapter is long. From 12,000 words a note under the word count says the review may take several minutes and uses more of your plan's limits or your API credit; from 40,000 words an orange box warns that the model may reach its output limit before it has written every suggestion, in which case the review fails and what it used still counts, and that a lower effort or shorter files help.
- The chapter list marks a conflict copy made by Obsidian Sync, Dropbox or Syncthing with a "conflict copy" badge on a line under its name, so the name keeps its full width. Point at the badge to see which tool made the copy and of which chapter. The copy is listed and opens like any other chapter; Lapidary never hides or deletes it.
- A chapter with merge markers, lines starting `<<<<<<<` or `>>>>>>>` that git or a sync tool leaves where two versions of the file disagreed, opens as it is, with a warning card that says so. The card also comes up when markers arrive while the chapter is open, as after a pull in Obsidian Git, and goes once they are taken out. Lapidary does not change the file. If markers arrive just as you press Save to chapter, nothing is saved, so you see them first; saving again goes ahead.
- Copy diagnostics, near the foot of the Setup screen, puts on the clipboard what someone helping you needs to know: the versions of Lapidary, Electron, the OpenAI library and your Claude Code; your review and display settings; what the last checks of Claude and ChatGPT found; how many chapters your manuscript folder holds, whether it is in an Obsidian vault, in git or in a cloud folder such as OneDrive, whether Obsidian Sync is on, and whether the guidelines file differs from the template; and the log's recent warnings, each as what failed and its error code, without the message, which can name a chapter. A Diagnostics window opens with it and shows exactly what was copied. It holds no chapter text, chapter names, email address or key, writes your user folder as `~`, and runs no check of its own, so it costs nothing.
- Updates from inside the app on Windows. Fifteen seconds after it starts, and once a day while it runs, Lapidary asks GitHub whether a newer version has been released; the request carries nothing about your chapters and no id of your copy, and Check for updates automatically, in About, turns this off. A card offers the new version with its size, and nothing is downloaded until you press Download; the same card then shows the download's progress and Restart now, and keeps its place among the other cards. Once it is downloaded, Restart now asks about anything not saved to the chapter as closing the window does, then says over the window that it is installing, closes Lapidary, runs the installer with its progress window and opens Lapidary again; while a review runs, it asks you to restart once the review has finished. Download and Restart now are also beside the update line in About, should you close the card. A download cut short by closing Lapidary, or done and not yet installed, goes on without asking again at the next check: 15 seconds after Lapidary next starts while Check for updates automatically is on, or when you press Check for updates. If Windows will not start the installer, a card says so and Lapidary stays open as it was; install the update from its release page, or close and reopen Lapidary, and the next check offers Restart now again. If the downloaded installer has gone, as when antivirus software removes it, Lapidary closes without installing it, and the next check downloads it again. On macOS and Linux the card points to the release page instead.
- About, from a new About button at the foot of Settings: Lapidary's icon, version and one-line description; Check for updates, with a line saying what the last check found (the button is greyed out while an update downloads, waits to be installed or is being installed, and after an install that failed, until Lapidary is opened again); Check for updates automatically, with what a check sends; What's new, which opens the release page of the version you have; Licences and notices, which opens the list of the software Lapidary includes with each one's licence; what Lapidary is and is not: it runs the Claude Code you installed, and can also review with ChatGPT through Sign in with ChatGPT; the reminder that suggestions come from an AI model and can be wrong; the versions of Electron, Chromium, Node.js, the OpenAI library and your Claude Code, with where Claude Code is installed; and Copy diagnostics, with Open log folder and Open data folder.
- Review with AI ends with a reminder under Start review: suggestions and comments come from an AI model and can be wrong or misleading, so check each one, and verify any factual claim, before you accept it.
- The Setup screen ends with Lapidary's name and version.
- When something goes wrong that Lapidary did not expect, a card says so, with Show diagnostics, at most once a minute. If the window cannot be drawn, a page takes its place that first writes your decisions and any open paragraph boxes, then offers Reload window, which has the keyboard focus, and Copy diagnostics; they are there when you open the chapter again. That page never saves to the chapter: a close the window was asking you about is called off, and the next close asks only if something is still being written or a review is running. If the window's process stops, a box offers Reload window or Quit; decisions and boxes are written as you make them, so only changes from the second before it stopped can be lost. What went wrong goes in the log, never the text of your chapters.
- Export HTML, in the Actions menu under Copy result, puts the text as it reads now on the clipboard as HTML to paste into a Royal Road chapter. Lines with no blank line between them stay close together, as in a status screen; headings, bold and italics, strikethrough, scene breaks, quotes and callouts, lists, tables and web links come across; `->` becomes an arrow, `--` and `---` dashes, and curly quotes straight ones. Obsidian's frontmatter, comments, tags, block IDs and footnote marks are left out (a `%%` or `<!--` that nothing closes shows as typed), an internal link keeps only its words, and an inline footnote's words go to the end. A code block comes across as its lines, inside a quote or callout too, and a callout with no title is titled with its type, as Obsidian shows it. The underline, subscript, superscript and line break tags typed in the text pass through; any other angle brackets and ampersands show as typed. Nothing but the clipboard is written.
- A Mode list at the top right of the chapter, with three views. Main is the review page as before. Full edit opens the whole chapter in one plain box, as it reads now, without the suggestions or comments, so you can go through it at once, with ProWritingAid for example; the frontmatter stands above the box and is left as it is. Done, or Ctrl+Enter, turns what you changed into edits of your own, already accepted, as a paragraph box's Done does, and nothing reaches the chapter until Save to chapter; Cancel, or Escape, goes back to Main, asking first if you typed anything. While Full edit is open the suggestions wait, and what you type in it is kept, as a paragraph box's text is, when you leave the chapter or the app closes. Preview shows the chapter as it reads now the way a Royal Road reader will see it, from the same HTML as Export HTML: the lines of a status screen close together, scene breaks, headings, quotes, lists and tables; links do not open and web images are not loaded. The review keys work in Main only, so in Preview the arrow keys scroll the page. Switching views with something typed and not applied asks first; Main and Preview each keep their place in the chapter, and Full edit opens at its top. Lapidary starts in Main, and a chapter where you left something typed in Full edit opens in Full edit with it.
- Sign in with ChatGPT, on a ChatGPT view of the Setup screen, which the Setup button on Settings' new ChatGPT account row opens. Lapidary opens OpenAI's own sign-in page in your browser; once you have signed in there and allowed Lapidary, the browser hands the sign-in back to Lapidary on your computer. Lapidary keeps the sign-in's tokens encrypted with your Windows login beside its settings, never shows them, and uses them only in its background process; beside them, unencrypted, it keeps your plan, whether you ticked the data settings box, and ids for your ChatGPT account, for Lapidary's registration with OpenAI and for your computer. The view and the row say whether you are signed in and, when the sign-in names it, your ChatGPT plan. While Lapidary waits for the browser, Cancel stops waiting; Use another account signs in with a different ChatGPT account; Sign out asks OpenAI to end the sign-in, then removes it, and says so if OpenAI did not confirm it. Lapidary never sees your ChatGPT password.
- A warning on the ChatGPT view that on personal ChatGPT plans OpenAI may use what you send to train its models unless you turn that off under Data controls in ChatGPT's settings, which Lapidary cannot check. Open Data controls opens that page in your browser, and the box I have confirmed my data settings is kept for each ChatGPT account; you may leave training on, but turning it off is recommended.
- Reviews with ChatGPT, on your ChatGPT plan, with no API key: choose ChatGPT in the Review menu, or in Review with AI's Provider list, once you have signed in with ChatGPT. A review goes to the model you choose from the ones your plan offers; nothing is billed per token. Lapidary renews the sign-in by itself as it runs out. When OpenAI no longer accepts it, the ChatGPT view opens and says your sign-in has ended, with Sign in with ChatGPT, rather than showing a failed review, and choosing ChatGPT while you are not signed in opens that view in place of the dialog.
- Before your first ChatGPT review, Review with AI shows the warning about training, with Open Data controls and the box I have confirmed my data settings, and the review cannot start until the box is ticked; it is kept for each ChatGPT account. With ChatGPT chosen, the dialog also shows your ChatGPT account, with Open Setup while you are not signed in.
- Your ChatGPT plan's usage in the status bar, in the same words as Claude's, when OpenAI's answers give it: from 80% of a limit, as in "5-hour limit 85% used, resets 15:45", and once a limit is reached. A ChatGPT review that stops at a usage limit puts up a card that says so, with when the limit resets if OpenAI said, and Manage usage, and the status bar says "ChatGPT: usage limit reached" until a review goes through. The status bar's ChatGPT label, such as "ChatGPT ready (Plus)", opens the ChatGPT view.
- After your first sign-in with ChatGPT, the ChatGPT view says "You're using your ChatGPT plan", with Manage usage and Got it.
- You can choose where backups are kept: Change beside Backups in Settings, or on the Backups folder row of the welcome guide's first step. Backups stay in the app's data folder unless you choose another folder, which can never be your manuscript folder or a folder inside it: Lapidary refuses one and says why, and refuses in the same way a manuscript folder that holds the backups folder, in a message that goes once you choose a folder it takes or another backups folder. Choosing another folder leaves the backups made so far where they were. If the backups folder cannot be made or written when you save, as on a drive that is not connected, nothing is written and a card says why, and Show backups and Show in folder say when the folder is not there.
- Full screen. F11, or the new button with two arrows at the right of the toolbar, before Settings, puts the window in full screen and back; its tooltip says which. Escape leaves full screen too, unless something else is open for it to close first, such as a dialog, a menu, the find bar or a suggestion's card, or you are editing a paragraph or the whole chapter. Escape now closes a suggestion's card, which before went only when the pointer left the words. Lapidary always starts in a window.
- Focus mode, for writing without distraction: a box in the App section of Settings, off unless you tick it, with how long leaving takes beside it, 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 seconds (10 to start with). With it ticked, going full screen keeps Lapidary on top of every other window, and after Alt+Tab, or a click on a window on another screen, it asks Windows for the focus back. Leaving full screen, or closing the window, then takes holding F11 or Escape for the time set: the window darkens under a count of the seconds in a ring that fills as you hold, and letting go early, any other key or a click goes back to your chapter, with nothing typed. Once the time is up, full screen ends, and a close goes on as usual, asking about anything not saved to the chapter. The full-screen button, Restart now and closing the window show the same count.
- Focus mode never holds you in: it ends by itself if Lapidary stops responding, if its window runs into an error it cannot draw past, or as Lapidary quits, and it cannot stop the Windows key or Ctrl+Alt+Del. A page, a folder or a file Lapidary opens for you opens behind it, so leave focus mode to use one. A change to the setting applies the next time you go full screen.

### Changed

- The view you chose in the Mode list stays when you open another chapter, from the list or with New chapter, or another review of the chapter from History or the list at the top of the side panel: Preview stays Preview, from the top of the chapter, and Full edit stays Full edit, opening on the chapter as it reads now. If you typed something in Full edit, you are asked first: for another chapter, as before, whether to keep it for later or apply and save it; for another review, whether to apply it in the review you are leaving, discard it or stay, where another review used to wait until you had finished or cancelled Full edit. A chapter where you left paragraphs you were editing opens in Main with them, from Preview too, and so does another review of the chapter while you have such paragraphs open; Lapidary still starts in Main.
- The Mode list is now the first thing in the toolbar, at its left end before the eye and the category chips, where it used to stand on a strip of its own above the chapter, so the chapter starts at the top of its column again. In a narrow window it gets smaller along with the chips. The Tab key reaches it first.
- Full edit is drawn as the Preview page, and you type on the page: the same paper, width, margins, typeface and line spacing, with no box or border round the text. The page grows with the chapter and scrolls as Preview does, and its edge turns a faint blue while you are typing in it. The hint, Cancel and Done stay at the foot of the column, under the page, so they are always in reach however far down the chapter you are. It is still a plain text field, so ProWritingAid works in it as before.
- The welcome guide starts with your manuscript folder, the only step Start needs, with two optional rows under it for where your reviews and backups are kept, each in the app's data folder unless you change it. Connecting an AI service is now the second step, Connect AI agents, marked optional, with a part for Claude and one for ChatGPT whichever you review with: Set up Claude, and Sign in with ChatGPT, which opens the ChatGPT view of Setup and starts the sign-in there. Use ChatGPT instead and Use Claude instead are gone: pressing either part's button while the other service is not connected makes it the one Review with AI starts with. Fill in your style sheet is the third step, also marked optional, and the line beside Start names the optional steps not done yet.
- The Setup screen has a Done button at its foot, for Claude and for ChatGPT, as well as Close at its top, and Escape now leaves it too. All three take you back to where you came from: to Review with AI, opened again with the keyboard focus on Start review, when you came from its Open Setup; to the welcome guide, on that service's button (Set up Claude, Sign in with ChatGPT or Set up ChatGPT, whichever it shows now), when you came from the guide; and otherwise to your chapter. Pressing the service's name in the status bar, or a Setup button in Settings, while Setup is open does not change where they take you. Once you have signed in with ChatGPT, or a check finds Claude ready after something needed doing, the keyboard focus moves to Done, and the page scrolls just far enough to show it when it is below the bottom of the window.
- Review with AI is as plain with ChatGPT as with Claude: the same rows, with your ChatGPT account added, and no notes on your plan's usage or on how the effort is sent. Open Setup stands beside the account only while you are not signed in. The warning about training still shows until you tick its box. "Using ChatGPT plan", with Manage usage, is on the ChatGPT view instead, under "Signed in to ChatGPT", once you have pressed Got it on the welcome after your first sign-in; Manage usage is also on the card for a usage limit reached.
- The toolbar's Review menu has an item for each AI service, Claude and ChatGPT, in place of With AI, then Manual under a line. Each opens Review with AI with that service chosen. Copy diagnostics has a ChatGPT part: whether you were signed in at the last check, your plan and the last failure, never a token or your account.
- Lapidary never writes over a `secrets.json` it could not read, such as one another program holds open: signing in or out of ChatGPT and the data settings box change nothing in it and say why. A damaged `secrets.json` is copied aside as `secrets.json.corrupt-<time>` before a new one is written, as the settings file is.
- Opening another chapter while the page has changes not saved to the chapter, or a paragraph box you have typed in, asks first: "Save 3 changes first", Keep for later or Cancel, with Keep for later as the answer Enter gives. Keep for later keeps your decisions in the review and the paragraphs you are editing, which open again when you come back to the chapter; leaving used to offer only to throw open paragraph boxes away. A chapter you make with New chapter asks the same before it opens. Nothing is asked when everything is saved. Clicking the chapter or the review already open no longer reloads it, so your place, the filters and the find bar stay as they were; a change to the chapter file that the app did not notice shows once you open another chapter and come back.
- A chapter opens with the review you last had on screen for it, which is almost always the newest; a new review takes its place as soon as it arrives. A list at the very top of the side panel shows the chapter's reviews, with their date, provider and model, how many suggestions are pending and how many changes are not saved to the chapter, the one on screen selected. When that one is not the newest, a warning mark stands beside the list; point at it to see when the newest is from. Choosing another review in the list, or in History, opens it at once without asking anything: your decisions are kept in each review as you make them, and a paragraph you are editing opens again there if it reads the same, or what you typed is kept in a text file you can open. The orange dot, Save to chapter and the questions go by the review on screen, so changes you leave unsaved in another review are counted on its line in the list. After a choice, j, k, a and the arrow keys work on the suggestions straight away; the arrow keys do not change the list while it is closed, so open it with Alt+Down to choose from the keyboard. Opening another review from History used to ask first when something was not saved to the chapter, and clicking the chapter while an older review was on screen opened a different one.
- Closing the window asks first when something would be left behind: changes not saved to the chapter, a paragraph box you have typed in, a review that is running, or text typed in a dialog. "Save 3 changes and close" writes them into the chapter first, "Close, keep for later", the answer Enter gives, keeps your decisions in the review and your paragraph boxes for when you come back, and Cancel keeps the window open. A minimised window comes back up to ask, and the question appears over the Setup screen too. Nothing is asked when everything is saved; the window then closes once your decisions are written, including any you make while it waits for them. Closing used to go at once, and a decision made just before it could be lost. If your decisions are still being written after five seconds, or could not be written, the app says so and offers Wait or Close anyway; if the app has stopped responding, a box says so and offers the same.
- Review with AI, when the page has changes not saved to the chapter, offers "Save 3 changes, then review", which writes them into the chapter and then starts, so the review reads them, and "Review without them", which asks first and says they stay in the old review, to be reopened from History. The dialog used to warn and offer Save to chapter, and Start review went ahead without them. Submit manual review asks the same before it builds: "Save 3 changes, then build" (the answer Enter gives), Build without them, or Cancel. When that save does not go through, nothing is sent or built, and the dialog stays open and says why. With nothing unsaved, both dialogs work as before.
- Escape or Cancel in a paragraph box you have typed in asks before your text is thrown away, with Keep editing as the answer Enter gives. A box you have not changed still closes at once.
- Save to chapter with nothing to save says "The chapter already matches the page." and leaves the file untouched, its modified time included; it used to write the same text again. It no longer asks about paragraph boxes you opened but did not change.
- The card shown when the chapter changed on disk now also says how many decisions changed to match the file, as when you made or undid a suggested change in Obsidian. So does the card Save to chapter shows when it finds the chapter changed.

### Added
